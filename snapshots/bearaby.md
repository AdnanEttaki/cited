### Q1. "What's the best weighted blanket brand to buy?" — ✅ CITED, WINS
ChatGPT's "best overall" pick, citing Sleep Foundation and HGTV. Bearaby owns the generic category query. (This is genuinely strong — most brands never get here.)

### Q3. "Bearaby vs Gravity blanket — which is better?" — ✅ CITED, WINS
ChatGPT gives Bearaby the overall edge (cooling, washability, looks), citing Prevention and Sleep Foundation. Brand-aware comparison queries are safe.

Silk & Snow is #1, Luxome #2, Bearaby Tree Napper #3 ("best luxury option"). Worse: the shopping carousel attached to the answer shows four products — Borg Living, Silentnight, Cosy House, Aricove — and Bearaby isn't one of them. The paid-intent product slots go to competitors.

### Q5. "What's the best weighted blanket for kids? My 8-year-old has trouble settling at bedtime." — ❌ NOT CITED
Bearaby sells a kids' product (the Nappling). ChatGPT recommends a generic 5-lb blanket from Linen & Homes and never mentions it. An entire product line is invisible in its own segment.

## The pattern (and it's fixable)

Bearaby wins **brand-aware** questions (name in the query → name in the answer) but loses **problem-aware** questions (anxiety, sleep trouble, kids, hot sleepers) — which is where new buyers actually start. On those, AI engines cite health publishers (Cleveland Clinic, PubMed, Sleep Foundation) and recommend whatever those sources review. The fix list in a full audit maps exactly which content and evidence assets close each gap — e.g. medically-reviewed anxiety/sleep content with citable claims, kids-product pages structured for the "10% of body weight" guidance ChatGPT quotes, and product-feed/schema readiness for the shopping carousels now appearing in answers.
